## Performance pass — 11 September 2026 (Claude)

Phone frame statistics (read-only `dumpsys gfxinfo` of the user's running Orbit process, 13,405 frames): 15.2 % janky frames, p50 8 ms, p90 17 ms, p99 42 ms, 1,663 slow draw-command frames. Raw results: `verification/perf-measurements/`, summary `verification/perf-result.json`.

Findings and changes — no visual change intended, nothing removed (checked backups in `verification/before-music-switch-20260910-234519/`):

1. **The deck fold repainted all four card shells on every frame.** Any per-frame change on a `.stack-card` (transform, opacity, clip-path, or an inherited custom property) re-resolved and repainted its `::before` shell — grain image, gradients, shadows — about 430 times per open/close. Each card now sits in a `.stack-motion` box that carries the per-frame transform, opacity, z-order and the fold clip (`overflow:hidden` plus height, the same square-bottom cut). The shell is a real child element `.stack-shell` with the identical declarations (the `.stack-card:before` rules were renamed) and its fold scale is set inline. Staged-reveal opacity/translate, the footer fade and the metric-icon fade are written inline to the exact elements instead of `--reveal`, `--details` and `--open` on the card; card children are compositor-promoted. Per two open/close cycles: style 864 → 165 ms, repaint 2,329 → 181 ms, main-thread busy 46 % → 25 %; shell repaints 428 → 16 (the two class toggles).
2. **Progress variables were set on ancestors of everything.** `--deck-progress` (was on the deck) and `--island-open` (was on the island) are set on the live bar and the island trigger — the only ancestors of their reader, the chevron — so the frost, shell, body and cards are no longer re-styled per frame. The hero's `--orb-y/--orb-scale/--readout-scale` are written as the same transforms directly on the canvas and readout. Style resolutions per fold cycle 14,121 → 2,576.
3. **Hidden chart work on every tap and metric change.** The daily and weekly charts live in folded cards that are invisible until the deck opens; `renderBars/renderLine` now run in idle time, or synchronously the moment the deck starts opening (tap or drag) and whenever it is open. `verification/check.cjs` exposes `renderCharts` and flushes it before its NaN assertions.
4. **Orb draw calls.** The 646 dots were 646 separate fills per frame; they are batched into 16 depth bands (same dots and radii; alpha quantised to 0.04 steps). `check.cjs` still counts 646 arcs.
5. **Live bar.** Whole-pixel shell radius while it grows (31 → 28 px in integer steps rather than a repaint per frame) and one forced layout in `measureDeck` instead of four: style 246 → 134 ms, repaint 409 → 284 ms per two open/close.

Not changed, because it is the accepted material: frost re-blur (`backdrop-filter`) while content moves beneath the live bar, the facts card frost and the utility island. GPU composite time is unchanged (about 2.9 s per two fold cycles at 4× throttle); reducing it would mean smaller blur radii or fewer frosted layers.

## Music switching fix — 10 September 2026 (Claude)

Root cause of the "goes dark, then loads" track change: most players publish a new track's title first and attach its cover a moment later. The native bridge finished its first artwork pass with nothing, reported `art:""`, and the page removed the cover and the `music-focus` tint in that same frame; the real cover then arrived and faded in from the bare page.

Changes (checked backups in `verification/before-music-switch-20260910-234519/`):

- `android/src/com/mani/orbit/MusicSession.java`: after a metadata change the artwork worker keeps re-reading the same track's metadata every 250 ms for up to 2 s while no cover is available (`ARTWORK_GRACE_MS`). During that time `read()` still reports `artLoading:true` and omits `art`, so the page keeps the previous cover. Null metadata no longer reports "no artwork" instantly. A different track's metadata still starts its own pass immediately.

- `verification/focus-music.html`: new assertions — a track change with pending artwork keeps the previous cover; an art-less track fades the cover out and releases it.
