## Edge-local lens on the indicator — 12 September 2026 02:21 (Claude)

The user's reaction to the first pass was that it still looked the same, with a reference image of a frosted bar carrying a bright refractive blob. That was a fair reading: frost alone diffuses, and on Orbit's near-black panels a diffusion of nothing looks like nothing.

**The engine can refract.** `verification/perf-tools/refraction-probe.cjs` renders a checkerboard under three capsules — blur only, blur plus an SVG displacement filter, and opaque — and measures the pixels. `backdrop-filter: url(#filter)` is accepted *and produces displaced pixels*: the blurred-only capsule flattens the pattern to a mean 147.2 with deviation 12.6, while the refracted one keeps visible structure at deviation 15.59 with the pattern bent toward the edges. Evidence: `verification/frosted-system/refraction/probe.png` and `probe.json`. This is an SVG `feImage` + `feDisplacementMap` path in the WebView's own compositor — **not** AGSL, not a native lens, and not a shader.

**What shipped.** One shared `#glass-lens` filter is inlined in the page: the displacement map's normal is neutral through the middle and deviates only in a tenth of the shape at each edge, at scale 0.07 of the bounding box, so the background bends where the glass curves and stays put in the centre. There is no interior magnification and no channel splitting. `.has-glass-lens` is set only when the engine accepts a filter reference in a backdrop filter; without it the indicator keeps the plain material layer, and the no-backdrop, reduced-transparency and forced-colours fallbacks all clear the lens explicitly.

With the lens the indicator's own diffusion drops to 3 px so the background stays readable through it, its tint drops back because the glass now carries the material, and its edge gains a brighter shaped rim. The launcher shortcuts take the same lens as a press treatment — they are actions, not a persistent selection, so the glass belongs to the press and nothing implies a selected tab.

**What this does not change.** Glass shows what is behind it. Behind Orbit's Body selector there is a hero glow and a ring; behind the reference's blob there is album art. The places where this now reads like the reference are the timer capsule over artwork and the launcher over the Home scene; a selector on a flat charcoal panel still cannot show much, and no filter changes that.

Verification: all existing checks plus `frosted-system` PASS at 390, 384 and 320 px, with the fixture now asserting that the indicator is either a verified lens (a filter reference, its filter present in the page, and its own blur light enough to see through) or a plain material layer — never an unverified second blur. Evidence re-captured at all three widths.

There is **no refraction or displacement pass**: this is CSS frost with shaped edge light, not an AGSL lens, and nothing here claims otherwise. ## Hard design constraints

1. **Keep the agreed identity.** The base app is black/charcoal with quiet silver/grey detail and a purple highlight. The user explicitly rejected changing the whole palette to a purple gradient. Frost references describe the material, not permission to copy their pastel colours. Actual album artwork may colour the focused music screen. Preserve the existing Orbit stack and font instead of introducing another visual system.

2. **Use the screen properly.** The phone app fills the display and hides system bars for the requested immersive view. Do not bring back a mock phone frame, surrounding black gutters or a disconnected framed app surface. The app background continues under the floating frosted bar; content remains scrollable clear of it. Supporting controls must remain readable and reachable on narrow screens.

3. **Make data clean and legible.** Lead with a useful visual summary, clear values and short supporting labels. The user rejected packed columns, awkward overlapping curved edges, heavy outlines and an overworked glass material. Use restrained frost, simple rounded panels and spacing to group information; avoid unnecessary dividing lines and decorative containers. They disliked log-first Body pages and a collection of repetitive full-page data lists. Relevant detail still needs to be available behind deliberate interaction.

4. **Keep full, purposeful motion.** The user explicitly rejected automatic reduced-motion suppression. Do not silently restore OS-preference gates. Keep manual orb pause and hidden/offscreen cleanup. Taps, swipes, selection pills, focus entry/exit and opening controls need visible, coordinated feedback. Follow the supplied Apple motion reference for continuity, timing and movement that can reverse from its current position. Fast snaps, remounting/shuffling content and background flicker are failures.
