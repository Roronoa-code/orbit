# Phone–Watch transport policy

Orbit retains Samsung imports, phone recordings and Watch journals in app-private local storage. That describes storage, not the entire transport path. Orbit uses Android's Wear Data Layer to synchronize its paired apps; it does not operate an Orbit cloud backend for these readings.

**Policy:** retain the platform's available routing, including its relay, to support disconnection/reconnection and background delivery. According to the [official Data Layer overview](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/data/overview), Google Play services normally uses the Bluetooth connection and may automatically route through Google's servers over Wi-Fi/LTE when Bluetooth is unavailable. Google documents cloud-relayed payloads as end-to-end encrypted; Bluetooth uses the platform-managed encrypted channel. Matching package names and signing certificates restrict delivery to the corresponding apps. Orbit additionally validates peer role/ownership and incoming payloads at its existing boundaries.

This is not a Bluetooth-only or network-free policy. Local-first storage and no Orbit server do not mean that Google infrastructure is never involved. Do not label `Node.isNearby`, a local database or a successful enqueue as proof of the actual transport route, remote receipt or durable commit. Critical workout controls remain live, expiring commands; persisted observations use the existing journals/ACKs, and replaceable phone health context uses Data Layer's latest-item cache. Routing policy must not weaken those distinct completion guarantees.

This policy preserves existing behavior; no transport or encryption replacement is introduced. The source was checked on 15 September 2026. It describes the platform contract, not a packet-level audit or successful paired-device test. Pairing/authentication and physical delivery qualification remain open.
